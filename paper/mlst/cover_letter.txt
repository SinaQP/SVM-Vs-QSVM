Subject: Benchmark — A Controlled Empirical Comparison of Classical and Quantum Kernel SVMs for Breast Cancer Classification

Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript “A Controlled Empirical Comparison of Classical and Quantum Kernel SVMs for Breast Cancer Classification” for publication as a Benchmark article in Machine Learning: Science and Technology. Benchmark is the intended submission type based on the journal's current definition of comparative evaluations on a consistent problem or dataset, subject to editorial and submission-portal classification.

Quantum kernels provide a principled way to use quantum state overlaps as similarity measures for classical machine-learning tasks. Their empirical value, however, can only be assessed against carefully selected classical comparators and under evaluation protocols that isolate preprocessing and hyperparameter selection from held-out testing. The submitted study addresses this methodological need through a controlled comparison of linear and radial-basis-function support vector classifiers with quantum support vector classifiers (QSVCs) on the Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer benchmark.

The experiment uses five predefined stratified 80/20 splits of the 569-sample dataset. Within each outer-training set, preprocessing and quantum Gram matrices are recomputed fold-locally, while QSVC repetition count, entanglement topology, and regularization are jointly selected by five-fold inner cross-validation. Each frozen choice is refitted on the complete outer-training set before one outer-test evaluation. Complementary exploratory analyses examine feature-map sensitivity, sample-size behavior, kernel geometry, and measured execution cost; they do not select the final architecture.

Under these conditions, the inner-selected classical comparator had higher malignant-class F1 on all five matched splits. Mean F1 was 0.934 versus 0.868 in two dimensions and 0.949 versus 0.872 in four dimensions. The two-sided exact Wilcoxon tests yielded raw p-values of 0.0625 and Holm-adjusted p-values of 0.1875. We therefore present the paired results as descriptive evidence, not population-level confirmation. The manuscript discloses that the outer splits overlap and that the work remains post hoc despite outer-test-isolated final selection.

The architecture and geometry analyses add context beyond a model leaderboard. Within the tested four-qubit full-entanglement maps, malignant-class F1 decreased from approximately 0.872 at one repetition to 0.540 at three repetitions. We describe this association conservatively as concentration-like behavior rather than proof of an asymptotic concentration mechanism. Centered kernel alignment with the selected RBF kernels was lower for the four-qubit comparison than for the two-qubit comparison (0.338 versus 0.573), yet the more divergent kernel geometry did not improve predictive performance. Fixed-hyperparameter learning curves likewise showed no small-data QSVC advantage in this setting. Runtime results are explicitly limited to the recorded CPU statevector implementation and are not presented as estimates of physical quantum-hardware performance.

The contribution is not a new quantum algorithm or a universal claim about quantum learning. It is a reproducible negative-result benchmark that combines nested model selection, paired multi-split reporting, feature-map ablation, sample-size analysis, and kernel-geometry diagnostics while making the statistical and experimental limitations visible. We believe this combination is relevant to MLST readers working on quantum machine learning, empirical methodology, and reproducible comparisons of emerging computational technologies.

The public repository is available at https://github.com/SinaQP/SVM-Vs-QSVM. The published submission-associated archival release is v1.1.0 (https://github.com/SinaQP/SVM-Vs-QSVM/releases/tag/v1.1.0); it contains the corrected nested methodology, authoritative outputs, configurations, predictions, and validation scripts supporting this submission. Historical release v1.0.0 remains preserved for provenance. The manuscript and supplementary material identify the benchmark as non-clinical and distinguish exact statevector simulation from physical QPU execution.
