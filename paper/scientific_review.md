## 1. Executive Review Summary

This manuscript presents a controlled empirical benchmark comparing classical Support Vector Machines (Linear and RBF kernels) against Quantum Support Vector Classifiers (QSVC with second-order Pauli-Z expansion feature maps) on the Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer (WDBC) dataset. The empirical pipeline features notable methodological strengths: strict outer-test isolation across 5 fixed random seeds, inner 5-fold cross-validation for hyperparameter tuning, fold-specific preprocessing refitting to prevent leakage, circuit depth and entanglement ablation across 60 runs, sample-size scaling from $N=50$ to $455$, and Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) analysis against tuned RBF kernels.

The empirical findings are transparent: classical SVMs consistently outperform QSVC across all evaluated dimensions (Malignant F1: $0.934$ vs. $0.868$ in 2Q; $0.949$ vs. $0.872$ in 4Q; 5/5 wins on matching seeds). Deeper quantum circuits show pronounced performance degradation, and sample-size scaling confirms no small-data quantum advantage.

However, from the perspective of an expert reviewer in quantum information, kernel methods, and experimental statistics, several critical theoretical, statistical, and conceptual formulations in the draft require immediate revision before submission to a premier journal.

## 2. Major Scientific and Methodological Concerns

### Major Concern 1: Operator Feature Space vs. Hilbert State Space Dimensionality (Section 11)
* **Problem:** In Section 11, the manuscript explains effective rank by stating:
  *"In the 2-qubit space, statevectors reside in a $2^2 = 4$-dimensional complex Hilbert space... effective rank of $7.35$."* and *"In the 4-qubit space, the Hilbert space expands to $2^4 = 16$ dimensions... effective rank to surge to $96.49$."*
* **Scientific Flaw:** In linear algebra, a Gram matrix generated by inner products of vectors in a $d$-dimensional vector space can have rank at most $d$. If a reviewer assumes the feature map is purely the statevector $|\psi(\mathbf{x})\rangle \in \mathcal{H} = \mathbb{C}^{2^n}$, an effective rank of $7.35$ in 2Q (where $2^2=4$) or $96.49$ in 4Q (where $2^4=16$) would appear mathematically impossible or indicative of a computational error.
* **Theoretical Reality:** The fidelity kernel is $K(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = |\langle \psi(\mathbf{x}) | \psi(\mathbf{x}') \rangle|^2 = \text{Tr}[\rho(\mathbf{x}) \rho(\mathbf{x}')]$. It evaluates the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product between density operators $\rho(\mathbf{x}) = |\psi(\mathbf{x})\rangle\langle\psi(\mathbf{x})|$ in the operator space $\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$, or equivalently the state space $\mathcal{H} \otimes \mathcal{H}^*$. The dimension of this linear operator space scales as $(2^n)^2 = 4^n$ (specifically $4^n - 1$ for traceless Hermitian matrices).
  - For $n=2$ qubits: $4^2 = 16$ operator dimensions. An effective rank of $7.35 \le 16$ is fully legitimate.
  - For $n=4$ qubits: $4^4 = 256$ operator dimensions. An effective rank of $96.49 \le 256$ is completely consistent.
* **Required Revision:** The manuscript must explicitly define the feature space of the fidelity kernel as the $4^n$-dimensional operator feature space and explain why effective ranks above $2^n$ are mathematically sound and expected.

### Major Concern 2: Kernel Concentration Terminology and Causal Attribution (Section 8 & Section 15)
* **Problem:** Portions of the manuscript describe the performance drop in deeper circuits as being "caused by exponential kernel concentration" or suggest that the study "demonstrates exponential concentration."
* **Scientific Flaw:** The study evaluated only two qubit dimensions ($n \in \{2, 4\}$) and circuit depths $\text{reps} \in \{1, 2, 3\}$. It did not prove an asymptotic concentration theorem, nor did it measure exponential scaling across large $n$. In 4Q, the off-diagonal mean was $0.096$ with standard deviation $0.101$, which reflects state dispersion but is not mathematically identical to Haar-random asymptotic concentration to a delta function.
* **Required Revision:** Adhere strictly to conservative terminology: "concentration-like behavior," "geometry consistent with concentration phenomena," and "associated with changes predicted by kernel concentration theory." Eliminate any statements asserting that the study "proves" exponential concentration or that concentration was the proven sole cause of classification failure.

### Major Concern 3: Statistical Framing, Sample Overlap, and Bootstrap Qualification (Section 14)
* **Problem:** Presenting bootstrap confidence intervals $[+0.0388, +0.0977]$ alongside a Wilcoxon test with $p=0.0625$ ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.1875$) risks misleading readers into believing classical superiority is formally statistically significant.
* **Scientific Flaw:**
  1. $n=5$ outer splits has a mathematical floor of $p = 2 \times (0.5)^5 = 0.0625$ for a two-sided exact Wilcoxon test. No test with $n=5$ can reach $\alpha = 0.05$.
  2. The 5 outer splits share ~60% of their training instances with one another. They are dependent, overlapping splits, not independent experimental trials.
  3. The bootstrap resampling was conducted across only five observed paired differences. Resampling 10,000 times from 5 numbers does not generate new statistical power.
* **Required Revision:** Explicitly label bootstrap intervals as "exploratory split-level percentile bootstrap intervals." State clearly that the five random splits are not independent experimental replicates. Maintain the conclusion as "consistent descriptive empirical evidence" rather than "formal statistical significance."

### Major Concern 4: Computational Complexity and Asymptotic Scaling Inferences (Section 13)
* **Problem:** Text discussing runtime scaling might imply that the jump in simulation time from 2Q ($0.226$s) to 4Q ($0.660$s) proves exponential computational scaling.
* **Scientific Flaw:** Two empirical data points ($q=2$ and $q=4$) cannot establish an exponential asymptotic scaling law. The ~2.9x increase in simulation wall-clock time reflects statevector linear algebra overhead ($2^2=4$ to $2^4=16$ dimensions) on a specific classical CPU implementation.
* **Required Revision:** Rigorously separate: (a) measured wall-clock runtime, (b) classical quadratic programming complexity ($\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ to $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$), (c) statevector simulation complexity ($\mathcal{O}(N \cdot 2^q + N^2 \cdot 2^q)$), and (d) physical quantum circuit evaluation scaling. Clearly state that two points illustrate simulation cost but cannot prove asymptotic complexity.

## 3. Minor Scientific and Presentation Concerns

1. **Secondary Endpoints and Statistical Claims:** Ensure that Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and ROC-AUC are consistently described as descriptive secondary endpoints and never referred to as "statistically significant."
2. **Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) Interpretation:** In Section 12, clarify that the lower CKA in 4Q ($\approx 0.338$) demonstrates that the quantum kernel induces a geometry substantially distinct from classical RBF, but explicitly emphasize that geometric novelty did not translate into improved predictive performance. Avoid any phrasing that could be interpreted as calling the 4Q kernel a "better representation."
3. **Biomedical Scope and Framing:** The manuscript must remain strictly framed as a methodological benchmark on a tabular dataset. Reinforce that findings do not constitute clinical validation and cannot be generalized to patient care or diagnostic pipelines.
4. **Abstract Length and Focus:** The Abstract should be concise (~180–250 words), focusing on motivation, core experimental design, key numerical findings, statistical power boundary, and realistic conclusions.
5. **Introduction Structure:** Add a dedicated, structured "1.1 Summary of Contributions" subsection to clearly outline the paper's specific contributions and research questions.
